Basketball analysts often explain winning with Dean Oliver’s “Four Factors” (shooting efficiency, turnovers, rebounding, and free throws). My question has two parts: how much of a game’s winning margin (team score minus opponent score) do these factors explain, and how well can we predict the margin of a future game when only information available before tipoff is allowed?
The project has two stages. In Stage 1, I will fit a regression of winning margin on the Four Factors using stats from the same game. I will compare it to ridge, lasso, and elastic net with a few extra stats added (assists, steals, blocks, fouls, and possessions). I expect the Four Factors to explain most of the margin, since they come from the same box score that decides the score. So the more interesting part is which stats matter most. I also expect regularization to help very little because we have a lot of games and not many predictors.
Stage 2 is the main part. Here I will predict a game’s margin using only information from before the game. I will use each team’s rolling averages from its earlier games that season, plus the AP ranking going into the game. The models are linear, ridge, lasso, and spline-based regression, with a random forest to compare against. I will tune them with cross-validation that only trains on earlier games, and I will compare test error to effective degrees of freedom to look at the bias-variance tradeoff. For the final test, I will train on earlier seasons and test on the most recent ones. I will compare against simple baselines like home court alone and each team’s past average margin. Since the data is observational, I will treat the results as associations and not causes.
